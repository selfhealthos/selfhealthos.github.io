Weight training gets its own page, body composition gets a real one, your meals turn up with the photo you took of them, and the Android app that has been feeding all of this is now in the repo.

## Gym

A new **Gym** page under Fitness, built on the sets you log on your phone.

The chart is **tonnage per session** — weight × reps, summed across the day. It's the headline number because session count and top weight both fail at the same question: *am I doing more work than I was?* A week of heavy triples and a week of light volume can show the same number of sessions and the same best lift while differing by half in total work.

Under it, a grid of working weights: exercises down, sessions across, **newest on the left**, each cell showing that day's tonnage with the sets beneath it. Reading one movement's weight backwards through time is what the page is for, and that wants the dates side by side rather than a card per day.

Two things it won't do. **Rest days are absent, not zero** — no bar, no column, because padding gaps with zeroes invents troughs you never lived and implies sessions that never happened. And **bodyweight work shows reps, not `0 kg`** — chin-ups carry no external load, so their tonnage really is zero, and printing it reads as a failed entry.

Gym sets also now appear on **Entries**, grouped into one card per exercise rather than thirty separate cards for a leg day.

See [Gym](/docs/features/gym) for the full guide.

## Body composition

**Body** has been rebuilt as the composition page, with weight moved over from the old "BP + Weight" (which is now just **Blood pressure**; the old link still redirects). Record weight, waist, hips, neck, body fat, height and a target — from the browser, backdated if you like.

Weight and BMI share a chart on two axes, which is honest here only because they're the same quantity in two units at a fixed height. Underneath, a colour-banded table scores weight, BMI, waist and waist-to-height against published thresholds — one row per day something was actually recorded, with nothing carried forward, so a weight repeated down the column can't colour days nobody stood on the scales.

**Waist-to-height leads the page.** It needs no scales, it catches central adiposity that BMI misses entirely in someone of normal weight, and the healthy limit is one number worth remembering: keep your waist under half your height.

The functional self-tests — grip, balance, sit-to-stand, dead hang — split out into their own **Tests** page.

## Photos on your entries

Meals and documents photographed in the Android app now upload to the portal and show their **thumbnail** on the Entries timeline and in the food log.

The picture is a separate upload from the entry itself, deliberately: the row lands with the rest of the sync batch and the image follows when it can, so a card is never hidden waiting on a photo that a bad connection is still pushing.

## Deleting entries

Anything logged in error can now be **deleted from its card** on the Entries page — the × in the top-right corner, then a confirm. It's the only write that timeline has ever had.

Deletions are tombstoned rather than erased. A hard delete would be invisible to your phone, which would cheerfully re-sync the entry straight back.

## Two new reports

**Seasons** averages every tracked metric by season, for the shifts that only surface across a year — how much less you move in winter, what the light does to your sleep.

**AI Prompt Report** renders your whole record as one markdown document with a copy box, ready to paste into ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude. **No LLM is called by the app or the server** — nothing here talks to an AI provider. The bet is that you're going to paste this somewhere anyway, so the page's only job is to make that paste complete. It defaults to all time, since a model asked for insight does better with more history. (If you'd rather a model read your data directly, that's still [MCP](/docs/features/mcp-integration).)

The **Work From Home** report has been redesigned as a per-metric card grid.

## Your own timezone

You can now set your timezone on your profile rather than inheriting the server's. On a shared instance, two people in different places were previously filing entries against one clock — which quietly put late-evening sessions on the wrong day for whoever wasn't where the server was.

## HTTPS without your own proxy

A new opt-in `compose.tls.yaml` overlay adds a TLS terminator holding a certificate you supply, for the LAN case: a phone posting a bearer token to a box on your wifi, where that token must not cross the network in the clear and no public CA can sign the name.

It's for people who *don't* already run a reverse proxy — if you do, `compose.proxy.yaml` still joins the frontend to your proxy's network instead. See [Reverse proxy](/docs/self-hosting/reverse-proxy).

## The Android app is in the repo

The Kotlin app that logs the gym sets, meals, photos and habits behind all of the above now lives in `mobile/` alongside the server. It syncs to your own portal and nowhere else.

This release also fixes two sync bugs worth naming: WFH days re-toggled after syncing minted a fresh id each time and were rejected by the server forever, and the workout player's focus mode has been reworked into a two-column layout.
