Two people, one room, one screen. Tick your friend's name in the workout player and every exercise you complete lands on both dashboards — their entries, their charts, their minutes, without them touching anything.

{/* truncate */}

## Friends

A new **Friends** page in the sidebar. Add someone by username, or by the short **friend code** on your own Friends page — read it out at the gym, paste it in chat, generate a new one whenever you like. They accept or decline from their own Friends page, and nothing happens until they accept.

There's no member list or user search, deliberately. On a shared instance, "who has an account here" isn't something a health app should publish, so lookups are exact-match only and a miss looks identical to a hit on someone who'd rather not be found by name. Friend codes are how you get found on purpose.

## Shared workouts

**Settings → Working out with friends** picks which friends show up as chips in the workout player — ten friends, one training partner, one chip. Tick whoever's actually with you and use the player exactly as before.

- Both of you get **one** entry each, even if you both press Complete on the same exercise.
- Entries say **who added them**, so a session you don't remember doing isn't a mystery.
- They're your rows — **delete any of them** like any other entry.
- **Time zones are per person**, so a late-evening session with someone abroad files under the right day for each of you.

## What friends can see

Nothing. That's not a simplification — a friend can add an exercise entry to your log when you train together, and that is the entire list. No entries, no metrics, no charts, no food log, no weight, not even who else you're friends with. The **Claude / MCP** token presets don't include the friends permissions either, so an MCP client on your instance can't enumerate your friends or write into anyone else's log.

You can switch off shared workouts entirely in Settings, and blocking someone stops them reaching you at all.

A shared timeline with likes and comments is [still planned](/docs/roadmap), and the friend graph was built with it in mind — but sharing what you *read* is a much bigger step than one narrow write, and it's getting its own visibility model rather than a flag bolted onto this one.

See [Friends & shared workouts](/docs/features/friends) for the full guide.
